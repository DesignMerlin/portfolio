<Section id="context" title="Context">

Immigify is an AI-powered immigration platform. I joined as its first product designer in April 2025 and owned everything a user could touch: the marketing website, the AI agent interfaces, the consultant CRM, and the multi-tenant SaaS dashboards on web and mobile. During my time there, the company raised a $1M pre-seed round.

The people at the centre of it are immigration consultants. They handle a high volume of cases where a missing document or a missed date has real consequences for someone's life. The product had to make that work calmer, not just faster.

</Section>

<Section id="problem" title="Problem">

Consultants were carrying the complexity of immigration compliance in their heads, with spreadsheets and inboxes as the backup. Every case moves through intake, document collection, preparation, filing and follow-up, and each stage has its own checklist and deadlines.

Two problems sat on top of each other:

- **The next action was hard to see.** Status fields said where a case was, not what needed doing next, or by whom.
- **AI help had to be trustworthy enough to act on.** In a legal context, an answer a consultant can't verify is worse than no answer.

<HMW>How might we turn complex compliance workflows into task-focused tools, with an AI agent consultants can trust enough to act on?</HMW>

</Section>

<Section id="reflection" title="Reflection">

I've learned that **when you're the only designer, the design system is your team.** Building the tokens and shared components early meant every new screen got easier, not harder.

And on AI: trust is designed, not assumed. The agent became useful the moment it started showing its working. That's now the first question I ask of any AI interface: can the person see why?

</Section>
