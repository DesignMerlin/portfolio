<Section id="context" title="Context">

Rent in Lagos is often paid a year or more up front, so sharing a flat is how a lot of young professionals, students and corps members make the city work. But the way people find a flatmate hasn't changed: a post in a WhatsApp group, a friend of a friend, or an agent who charges an inspection fee to show you a room that doesn't match the photos.

Room8 started from a simple observation: **the flat matters, but the flatmate decides whether you stay.** Every listing app helps you find a room. Almost none help you find out who you'll be living with.

</Section>

<Section id="problem" title="Problem">

People looking for a shared room are making two decisions at once, where to live and who to live with, but the tools available only support the first. The second gets made on instinct, at an inspection, under pressure to pay before someone else does.

<HMW>How might we help people choose a flatmate with the same confidence they choose a room?</HMW>

Three constraints shaped the answer from day one:

- **Price is the first filter, always.** Any design that makes people work to see the monthly cost will lose them.
- **Trust is fragile.** Fake listings and fee scams are common, so the app has to earn trust before it asks for anything.
- **Compatibility has to explain itself.** A number on its own reads like a horoscope. People need to see why.

</Section>

<Section id="decisions" title="Key decisions">

<DecisionLog
  items={[
    {
      title: "A match with reasons, not a swipe",
      context: "Matching apps default to a swipe deck, and it was the first idea in the room.",
      chose: "A match percentage on every listing card, with a you-versus-them breakdown on the detail screen, so the number is always explainable.",
      rejected: [
        { option: "A swipe deck of people", why: "It turns a year-long housing decision into a snap judgement on a face, and hides price and location, the two things people filter on first." },
        { option: "A hidden score that only says 'good match'", why: "Opaque. If people can't see why, they don't trust it, and they can't tell which habit to ask about at the visit." },
      ],
    },
    {
      title: "Lead every card with the monthly price",
      context: "Cards have to support fast comparison on a small screen.",
      chose: "Price first, then room type, area, host and match: the order people actually ask the questions in.",
      rejected: [
        { option: "Large photo-first cards", why: "Beautiful, but two listings per screen makes comparing slow." },
        { option: "Price revealed on the detail screen", why: "It only delays the first filter everyone applies." },
      ],
    },
    {
      title: "Chat stays in the app until both sides are ready",
      context: "Trust is the product. Phone numbers in public listings are how scams and harassment start.",
      chose: "Requests and conversations live in Chats. Contact details are shared only when both people choose to.",
      rejected: [
        { option: "Phone number on every listing", why: "Fastest to build, worst for safety, and it moves the relationship off the platform." },
      ],
    },
    {
      title: "Three intent tabs instead of a filter screen",
      context: "Most searches fall into a few intents.",
      chose: "Recommended, Nearby and Luxury as one-tap tabs, with a filter button for everything else.",
      rejected: [
        { option: "A single advanced filter sheet", why: "Powerful but slow; most people never open it, and it buries the common cases." },
      ],
    },
  ]}

The result is a complete mobile experience: onboarding with a compatibility profile, a budget-first home feed, listing details that explain every match, in-app requests and chat, and favourites for shortlisting. The compatibility model is the core idea, and it shows up everywhere a decision gets made.

<Section id="reflection" title="Reflection">

The biggest lesson was that **an explanation is a feature.** The match percentage only became useful once it could explain itself, and that changed how I think about any score in a product, AI-generated or not.

If I took Room8 further, I'd test the compatibility questions themselves. Which habits actually predict a good flatshare is a research question, not a design one. I'd also carry the answers into shared house rules after move-in, so the match keeps paying off after the decision.

</Section>
