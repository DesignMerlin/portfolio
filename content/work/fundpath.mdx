<Section id="context" title="Context">

When someone needs help paying for surgery, school fees or rebuilding after a flood, the appeal usually travels by WhatsApp: a photo, a story and a bank account number. People give, often generously. Then the trail goes cold. Donors rarely learn whether the target was reached, and fundraisers spend hours answering "did my transfer arrive?"

FundPath set out to make giving feel as trustworthy as it is generous. The brief came down to one question.

<HMW>How might we offer real-time updates of funds from donors, and make transparency the default?</HMW>

</Section>

<Section id="problem" title="Problem">

Crowdfunding runs on trust, and trust runs on evidence. The informal way of fundraising gives donors almost none:

- **No proof of arrival.** A bank transfer to a stranger has no shared record, and no receipt the donor can point to.
- **No proof of use.** Even when a target is reached, donors don't see what the money paid for.
- **No momentum.** Without a visible total, a campaign can't show that other people are already giving, which is one of the strongest reasons people give at all.

So the design problem wasn't "build a donate button". It was: show money moving, in real time, from the donor to the cause and out the other side.

</Section>

  items={[

      title: "Transparency is a tab, not a PDF",

        { option: "A downloadable report at the end of the campaign", why: "Arrives too late to build trust, and almost nobody opens it." },
        { option: "Organiser-written updates only", why: "Useful for the story, but not evidence. Updates should sit next to the numbers, not replace them." },
      ],
    },
    {
      title: "Give from a wallet, not straight to an account",
      context: "Bank transfers are familiar, but they leave no record on the platform.",
      chose: "An in-app wallet: top up once, donate in two taps, and every gift is recorded for the donor and the campaign at the same moment.",
      rejected: [
        { option: "Show the organiser's bank details", why: "Recreates the exact problem: no proof of arrival and no live total." },
        { option: "Card payment at every donation", why: "More steps for every gift, and it puts a payment form between the donor and the moment they decided to give." },
      ],
    },
    {
      title: "Show progress on every card",
      context: "Momentum drives giving, and urgency matters for medical campaigns.",
      chose: "Raised-versus-goal, a progress bar, donor faces and days left on every campaign card in every list.",
      rejected: [
        { option: "Progress only on the detail screen", why: "Hides the most persuasive and most honest information until after the tap." },
      ],
    },
    {
      title: "Confirm in a sheet that shows the wallet before and after",

      rejected: [
        { option: "One-tap donate with no confirmation", why: "Fast, but easy to send the wrong amount, and there's no moment to reassure the donor." },

FundPath came together as 30+ screens covering onboarding, discovery by cause, campaign pages with live activity and a spending ledger, the wallet, donation history with receipts, and starting a campaign with verification. Transparency stopped being a report and became part of the interface.

<Section id="reflection" title="Reflection">

I learned that **in money products, motion is evidence.** A number that counts up while a bar fills tells the donor something happened. The same update, applied instantly, reads like a page that refreshed. The interaction carries part of the trust.

Next, I'd design the fundraiser's side of updates in more depth. The emotional moment of thanking donors is underserved, and it's what turns a one-time gift into a returning donor.

</Section>
